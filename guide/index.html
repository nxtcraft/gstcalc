What is GST?

Goods and Services Tax (GST) is India's unified indirect tax system introduced on 1 July 2017. It replaced over 17 central and state taxes including central excise duty, service tax, VAT, and octroi — simplifying India's fragmented indirect tax system into a single, comprehensive structure.

GST is a consumption-based, destination-based tax — it is levied where goods or services are consumed, not where they are produced. This ended the cascading effect (tax on tax) that existed under the old system.

  • Dual structure: Both the central government and state governments simultaneously levy GST on the same transaction.
  • Input Tax Credit (ITC): Businesses can claim credit for GST paid on inputs, ensuring the tax burden falls only on the final consumer.
  • Four-tier rate structure: 5%, 12%, 18%, 28% — plus 0% for essentials and special rates for gold, diamonds.
  • GST Council: A joint body of central and state finance ministers that decides rates and policy.

GST Formulas — Add and Remove GST

There are two calculation modes depending on whether the price you have includes GST or not.

Mode 1 — Add GST (exclusive to inclusive):

  • GST Amount = Base Price × GST Rate ÷ 100
  • Total Price = Base Price + GST Amount

Example: Base price ₹1,000, GST rate 18% → GST = ₹180 → Total = ₹1,180

Mode 2 — Remove GST / Reverse GST (inclusive to exclusive):

  • Base Price = Inclusive Price ÷ (1 + GST Rate ÷ 100)
  • GST Amount = Inclusive Price − Base Price

Example: MRP ₹1,180 with 18% GST → Base = 1180 ÷ 1.18 = ₹1,000 → GST = ₹180

Common mistake: Do NOT calculate 18% of ₹1,180 to get the GST in an inclusive price. That gives ₹212.4, which is wrong. Always use the reverse formula: divide by (1 + rate/100).

CGST vs SGST vs IGST — The Complete Explanation

This is the most confusing part of GST for most people. The same GST rate applies differently depending on whether the transaction is within a state or between states.

Intra-state transaction (supplier and customer in the same state):

  • GST is split 50-50 between the Centre and the State.
  • CGST (Central GST) = half the GST rate → collected by central government
  • SGST (State GST) = half the GST rate → collected by state government

Example: A Maharashtra business sells a service at ₹1,000 (18% GST) to a customer in Maharashtra:
→ CGST = 9% of ₹1,000 = ₹90 (goes to Centre)
→ SGST = 9% of ₹1,000 = ₹90 (goes to Maharashtra government)
→ Total GST paid by customer = ₹180. Total invoice = ₹1,180.

Inter-state transaction (supplier and customer in different states):

  • Only IGST (Integrated GST) applies at the full rate.
  • IGST is collected by the central government and later apportioned to the destination state.

Example: Same Maharashtra business sells the same ₹1,000 service to a customer in Gujarat:
→ IGST = 18% of ₹1,000 = ₹180 (collected by Centre, later shared with Gujarat)
→ Total invoice = ₹1,180.

The final price paid by the customer is identical (₹1,180) in both cases. The CGST/SGST vs IGST split only determines which government receives the tax — it has no effect on the buyer's cost.

UTGST (Union Territory GST) applies instead of SGST for intra-UT transactions in Chandigarh, Puducherry, Lakshadweep, Daman & Diu, Dadra & Nagar Haveli, Andaman & Nicobar Islands.

Who Needs to Register for GST?

GST registration is mandatory for:

  • Businesses selling goods with turnover exceeding ₹40 lakh per year (₹20 lakh in special category states like Northeast, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand).
  • Service providers with turnover exceeding ₹20 lakh per year (₹10 lakh in special category states).
  • Interstate suppliers regardless of turnover — even one inter-state sale mandates registration.
  • E-commerce sellers who sell through platforms like Amazon, Flipkart, or Meesho — mandatory regardless of turnover.
  • Reverse charge mechanism (RCM) recipients — businesses that receive certain services from unregistered vendors.

Voluntary registration is possible even below threshold — useful for businesses wanting to claim input tax credit.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is GST calculated in India?

Add GST: Total = Base × (1 + Rate/100). Remove GST: Base = Total ÷ (1 + Rate/100). For 18% on ₹1,000: Total = ₹1,180. Reverse: ₹1,180 ÷ 1.18 = ₹1,000. Never compute GST% on the inclusive price directly — that overstates the GST amount.

What is the GST rate on services in India?

Most services are taxed at 18% GST. Exceptions: restaurants 5% (non-AC) or 18% (AC), economy air travel 5%, construction 12%, healthcare and education 0%. Banking and insurance: 18%. Professional services (CA, lawyer, consultant): 18%.

What is IGST and when does it apply?

IGST (Integrated GST) applies when the supplier and customer are in different states (inter-state supply). IGST = full GST rate collected by the central government, which then apportions the state's share to the destination state. For same-state transactions, CGST + SGST (each half) apply instead.

How do I calculate GST-inclusive price?

To find the original base price from an MRP that includes GST: Base = MRP ÷ (1 + GST%/100). Example: MRP ₹590 with 18% GST → Base = 590 ÷ 1.18 = ₹500. GST amount = ₹90. The GST calculator handles this in "Remove GST" mode automatically.

Is GST applicable on exports?

No. Exports are zero-rated under GST — the GST rate on exported goods and services is 0%. Exporters can claim a refund of all input GST paid on materials/services used in producing the exported goods. This is different from an exemption, where input credit cannot be claimed.
